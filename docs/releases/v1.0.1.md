Sir Thaddeus 1.0.1 broadens how we test the assistant and makes future
improvements easier to prove, compare, and keep.

## What changed

The benchmark program now measures more than whether the final answer happened
to look right. It also tracks whether Sir Thaddeus used the right capability,
produced a verifiable result, preserved state when no action should occur,
returned valid output, stayed within resource budgets, and avoided losing
previous safety, speed, context, and usability gains.

The new scorecard gives every important metric a clear definition, owner, gate,
and lifecycle. That makes it much harder for an apparent win to come from a
changed test, an incompatible run, a looser scorer, or a hidden tradeoff.

## A more useful test bank

The rebuild plan keeps the reviewed bank at 100 cases or fewer, but gives those
cases broader coverage. It includes:

- paired raw-model and unchanged-assistant controls;
- direct tasks plus paraphrases and changed entities or values;
- verified tool and state outcomes;
- missing-information and no-action safety cases;
- exact repeats before a candidate reaches disjoint validation;
- latency, context, model-call, token, and resource reporting; and
- retention checks for important gains that a single accuracy score can miss.

This structure is model-agnostic. Small models remain useful for exposing weak
contracts and routing seams, while larger models can show whether a limitation
comes from the harness or the model itself.

## What the latest testing taught us

The reviewed 64-case scorecard exposed useful weaknesses that the older headline
score did not show clearly. A narrow Wiki operation-safety candidate improved
the focused development and validation slices, but missed its absolute
promotion gate. We rejected the production change and kept the evidence.

That result narrowed the next research seam: the remaining misses are less about
recognizing that a Wiki operation exists and more about binding the correct
content and target. Future work can begin there without pretending this release
already solved it.

## What this release does not claim

Version 1.0.1 is primarily a measurement, documentation, and research-discipline
release. It does not present broader tests as a model-intelligence improvement,
and it does not ship the rejected experimental behavior.

The practical improvement is confidence: future benchmark gains should be more
meaningful, reproducible, and less likely to hide regressions elsewhere.

## Downloads and compatibility

The stable release workflow publishes self-contained full bundles and SHA-256
checksums for Windows x64, Linux x64, and macOS Apple Silicon. Windows remains
the primary polished desktop target, and users still provide their own local or
OpenAI-compatible model endpoint.
